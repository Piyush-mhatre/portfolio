Equity Stocks

What is Equity?

Equity, often called shareholder equity, is regarded as the sum of money that will be returned to the shareholders of a certain company if all of its assets are liquidated and the whole debt of that company is completely paid off.

Equity is displayed in the balance sheet of a company. It is one of the key indicators that an investor uses to identify a company's financial soundness.

In simpler terms, equity is the total amount of money that a shareholder is eligible to receive if all of a company's debts are paid off and its assets liquidated. When an individual invests in a company's equities, he/she becomes its partial owner.

What are Equity Shares?

Equity shares are long-term financing sources for any company. These shares are issued to the general public and are non-redeemable in nature. Investors in such shares hold the right to vote, share profits and claim assets of a company. The value in case of equity shares can be expressed in various terms like par value, face value, book value and so on.

Features of Equity Shares

Equity shares have the following characteristics, which make it one of the most popular investment tools in a stock market –

  1. Most types of equity shares include voting rights to an investor, allowing him/her to choose individuals responsible to run the business. Electing efficient managers allows a company to increase its annual turnover, thereby increasing investors' average dividend income.
  2. Equity shareholders are eligible to realise additional profits generated by a company in a fiscal year. This increases the total wealth of individual investors having a considerable investment in equity shares of a company.
  3. Even though equity shares are not repaid until a business closes down, equity shares already issued can be traded in the secondary capital market. Thus, investors can withdraw funds from a company upon their discretion. This ensures massive wealth creation through capital appreciation of such shares.

Why Should You Invest in Equity Shares?

Investing in best equity shares have the following benefits, such as –

High Income

Equity share market is an ideal segment of the capital market responsible for the remarkable income of investors. Wealth creation not only works through capital appreciation of such securities but also high dividend earnings received by individuals.

Hedge Against Inflation

Investment in profitable equity shares increases the standard of living of individuals through asset value appreciation. Money invested in equity shares offer manifold returns, higher than the rate of erosion of an individual's purchasing power due to inflation. Thus, the real value of investments tends to rise over time.

Portfolio Diversification

Investors having a low aptitude for risk tend to stick with debt instruments, as it is less volatile. However, stock and bond market fluctuations are inversely related when it comes to aggregate demand. Thus, when the bond market is underperforming, risk-averse investors can profit from investment in best equity shares through stock market investments.

What are the Risks Associated with this Investment?

Equity share market tends to be the most volatile segment in a stock market, profoundly affected by minor fluctuations. Returns on equity investments are paid out after all other obligations of a company have been met. During market downturn, production cycle of a business is affected, thereby reducing profits generated by a business. This lower share of profit is used up to meet all existing liabilities before funds are disbursed to as equity investment returns. Thus equity markets tend to be adversely affected during market downturn.

Market fluctuations are a part of the business cycle, which has associated highs and lows as per the prevailing socio-economic scenario of a country. Even if equity shares demonstrate lower returns at a certain point of time, it is bound to pick up when the economy recovers.

Also, equity investments tend to rise in value over time. Thus, if funds are kept locked-in for an extended period, the value of the same is bound to increase manifold in the future, thus ensuring substantial wealth accumulation of investors through capital gains.

Alternative Investment Options

Debt Securities

Equity shares yield the highest returns on total investment in the stock market. However, it has the highest amount of risks associated as well, which do not bore well with risk-averse investors. Alternative investment options in the form of debt instruments can be undertaken by them, which have lower risk burden. However, the returns generated by these securities tend to be smaller as well, reducing the chances of substantial capital gains.

How to Buy and Sell Stocks

You can buy and sell stocks through:

  • A direct stock plan
  • A dividend reinvestment plan
  • A discount or full-service broker
  • A stock fund

Direct Stock Plans

Some companies allow you to buy or sell their stock directly through them without using a broker. This saves on commissions, but you may have to pay other fees to the plan, including if you transfer shares to a broker to sell them. Some companies limit direct stock plans to employees of the company or existing shareholders. Some require minimum amounts for purchases or account levels.

Direct stock plans usually will not allow you to buy or sell shares at a specific market price or at a specific time. Instead, the company will buy or sell shares for the plan at set times — such as daily, weekly, or monthly — and at an average market price. Depending on the plan, you may be able to automate your purchases and have the cost deducted automatically from your savings account.

Dividend Reinvestment Plans

These plans allow you to buy more shares of a stock you already own by reinvesting dividend payments into the company. You must sign an agreement with the company to have this done. Check with the company or your brokerage firm to see if you will be charged for this service.

Discount or Full-Service Broker

Brokers buy and sell shares for customers for a fee, known as a commission.

Stock Funds

Stock funds are another way to buy stocks. These are a type of mutual fund that invests primarily in stocks. Depending on its investment objective and policies, a stock fund may concentrate on a particular type of stock, such as blue chips, large-cap value stocks, or mid-cap growth stocks. Stock funds are offered by investment companies and can be purchased directly from them or through a broker or adviser.

Equity vs Other Investments

  • Equity requires low investment: Unlike Fixed Deposit, Gold & Real estate, you can enter the equity market with a much smaller capital.
  • Equity offers higher returns: It is historically proven that Equity offers better returns in comparison to FD, Gold & Real-estate.
  • Returns on Equity beat inflation & are completely tax-free.
  • Equity offers high liquidity: Equity can be bought & sold very easily & converted to cash pretty fast.
  • Equity is the best performing asset class: Equity increases your wealth faster over a long time due to compounding effect, capital appreciation & dividend income.

What Is the Difference Between Stocks and Bonds?

Stocks are issued by companies to raise capital to grow the business or undertake new projects. There are important distinctions between whether somebody buys shares directly from the company when it issues them in the primary market or from another shareholder in the secondary market. When the corporation issues shares, it does so in return for money.

Bonds vary from stocks in several ways. Bondholders are creditors to the corporation and are entitled to interest as well as repayment of the principal invested. Also, creditors are given legal priority over other stakeholders in the event of a bankruptcy and will be made whole first if a company is forced to sell assets.

Conversely, shareholders often receive nothing in the event of bankruptcy, implying that stocks are inherently riskier investments than bonds.

Large Cap Mutual Funds

What are Large Cap Funds?

Large Cap Mutual Funds are equity funds that invest a bigger proportion of their total assets in companies with a large market capitalisation. These companies are highly reputed and have an excellent track record of generating wealth for their investors over a long period.

Large Cap Funds are hence known to generate regular dividends and steady compounding of wealth. Also, these schemes carry a lower risk as compared to the small-cap or mid-cap schemes and are known to generate steadier returns. They are a good option for investors with a relatively lower risk appetite and a long-term investment horizon.

According to SEBI, large cap companies fall in the top 100 of the list of companies according to market capitalisation. Hence, investing in these companies is considered to be less risky and steady.

Features of a Large Cap Fund

Before you invest in large cap funds, here are some things that you must consider:

  1. Risk and Return
    All equity mutual funds are affected by market conditions. When the benchmark of the scheme fluctuates, the Net Asset Value (NAV) moves up or down, too. However, unlike small-cap and mid-cap schemes, the NAV of a large cap fund does not fluctuate a lot. Hence, investing in large cap schemes offers stability to your investment portfolio. Having said this, the returns from these schemes are usually lower than the mid-cap or small-cap funds.
    Remember, you can consider investing in these funds if you desire stable returns at a lower risk exposure.
  2. Not for Short-Term Investors
    When the market slumps, large cap funds also experience underperformance in their portfolios. However, since the money is invested in financially strong companies, this underperformance averages itself out over a period of time.
    The general understanding is that if you stay invested for more than seven years, then you can expect returns of around 10-12%. Hence, these mutual funds are usually recommended to investors with a long-term investment horizon.
  3. Consider your Financial Goals
    Large-cap mutual funds carry a reasonable amount of risk and offer stable returns. Hence, many investors turn to these schemes when they are planning their investment for retirement.
    Also, investors who want to gain exposure to the equity markets without taking too many risks prefer investing in large-cap mutual funds. It is important to consider your financial goals before investing.

How Does a Large Cap Mutual Fund Work?

By now, you know that large cap funds invest in big organisations. These schemes try to offer regular dividends and capital appreciation in the long term. If you are a risk-averse investor but want to benefit from equity investments, then large cap equity funds are the best option available to you.

Since these schemes invest in financially strong large cap companies, they can withstand a slowdown in the markets. However, the returns are lower compared to mid-cap or small-cap funds.

In the long term (around five to seven years), these funds tend to offer good capital appreciation. However, before you start investing, ensure that you consider your investment objectives and risk tolerance apart from the long-term investment horizon.

These funds carry varied sets of benefits for the investor, and some of them are:

  1. Lower Risks
    The underlying companies of these funds are large capitalization companies, which are not majorly subject to market movements. They are already well-established companies that do not hold a lot of risks. Therefore, the investors of large cap funds will be investing in low-risk funds.
  2. Higher Returns than Bank Deposits

Taxation Rules of Large Cap Mutual Funds

Being equity funds, large cap mutual funds are subject to capital gains tax and dividend distribution tax.

Capital Gain Taxes

On redeeming the units of a large cap equity fund, you earn capital gains – which are taxable. The rate of tax depends on the holding period – the period for which you were invested in the fund.

  • The capital gains earned by you for holding a period of up to one year = Short Term Capital Gain (STCG), which is taxed at 15%.
  • The capital gains earned by you for a holding period of more than one year = Long Term Capital Gain (LTCG). LTCG up to Rs. 1 lakh is not taxable. Any LTCG above this amount is taxed at the rate of 10% without indexation benefits.

Dividend Distribution Tax

  • When a fund house pays dividends, it needs to deduct DDT of 10% at the source before making the payment.

Mutual Funds – Mid Cap

When it comes to investing in equity mutual funds, choosing the scheme based on the right market capitalization is key to meeting your investment objectives. In this section, we will explore Mid Cap Mutual Funds and their essential aspects.

What are Mid Cap Funds?

Mid Cap funds invest in equity and equity-related instruments of Mid Cap companies. According to the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI), Mid Cap companies are those ranked between 101 and 250 in terms of market capitalization.

Mid Cap companies fall between small-cap and large-cap companies, offering a blend of both advantages and disadvantages. These funds usually provide better returns than large-cap funds but are more volatile. They are also more stable than small-cap funds but tend to offer slightly lower returns.

In summary, Mid Cap mutual funds offer a balance between risk and return. With a well-selected stock portfolio and good diversification, investors can expect better returns than large-cap funds with a manageable level of volatility.

Features of a Mid Cap Fund

  • Fund Management: The performance of the fund manager and the fund house is crucial in ensuring good returns. A history of successful management during market volatility is important to consider.
  • Suitability for Young Investors: Mid Cap funds offer excellent compounding potential and are particularly suitable for younger investors who have time on their side.

How Does a Mid Cap Mutual Fund Work?

Mid Cap Mutual Funds carry a higher risk than Large Cap Funds. Hence, these funds are suitable for investors with a higher risk tolerance and an investment horizon of 8-10 years.

Mid Cap funds present ample opportunities for wealth creation, but selecting a scheme with robust market research and investment strategies is key. Investors should carefully assess their financial goals, risk preferences, and investment horizon before opting for Mid Cap funds.

Mutual Funds – Small Cap

What is Small Cap Fund?

Small-cap funds invest a major portion of their investible corpus into equity or equity-related instruments of small-cap companies. According to SEBI, these schemes need to invest at least 80% of their total assets in small-cap companies, defined as those ranked below the 250th in terms of market capitalization (typically less than ₹5,000 crores).

Small-cap funds carry a high level of risk due to their sensitivity to market volatility, but also have the potential to offer high returns over time. These funds are best suited for investors with high risk tolerance and a long investment horizon.

Features of Small Cap Equity Funds

How Does a Small Cap Mutual Fund Work?

While small-cap funds may be volatile in the short to medium term, they offer high growth potential in the long term. Share values of these companies can grow significantly, but the risks remain substantial.

Government Bonds

What Are Government Bonds?

Government bonds are debt securities issued by the central or state governments to finance public expenditures. They offer low-risk investment options backed by the sovereign guarantee. When you invest in a government bond, you're lending money to the government in exchange for periodic interest payments and the return of the principal amount at maturity.

Types of Government Bonds in India

  1. Treasury Bills (T-Bills): Short-term securities with maturities of 91, 182, or 364 days. They are issued at a discount and redeemed at face value, with no periodic interest payments.
  2. Dated Government Securities (G-Secs): Long-term bonds with maturities ranging from 5 to 40 years. These bonds offer fixed or floating interest rates and pay interest semi-annually.
  3. Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGBs): Bonds denominated in grams of gold, offering an interest rate of 2.50% per annum. The tenure is 8 years with an exit option after the 5th year.
  4. Floating Rate Savings Bonds (FRSBs): Bonds where the interest rates are linked to prevailing market rates, providing protection against interest rate fluctuations.
  5. 7.75% GOI Savings Bond: A taxable bond offering a fixed interest rate of 7.75% per annum, paid every six months.
  6. Inflation-Indexed Bonds (IIBs): Bonds that adjust both the principal and interest payments based on inflation, offering protection against rising prices.
  7. State Development Loans (SDLs): Bonds issued by state governments to finance their development projects, carrying the backing of the respective state government.

Benefits of Investing in Government Bonds

  • Capital Safety: Government bonds carry minimal risk of default as they are backed by the government.
  • Regular Income: They offer periodic interest payments, providing a steady income stream.
  • Tax Benefits: Certain bonds like SGBs offer tax exemptions on interest and capital gains if held until maturity.
  • Hedge Against Inflation: Inflation-indexed bonds and SGBs protect against inflation.
  • Liquidity: Government bonds are tradable on exchanges, providing liquidity to investors.

How to Invest in Government Bonds

  • Primary Market: Invest through RBI auctions or authorized banks and financial institutions during the initial offering.
  • Secondary Market: Buy and sell bonds through stock exchanges like NSE or BSE using a demat and trading account.
  • Online Platforms: Invest through online investment platforms and brokerage services.

Corporate Bonds

What Are Corporate Bonds?

Corporate bonds are debt securities issued by corporations to raise capital for various purposes, such as business expansion, capital improvements, or debt refinancing. Investors who purchase these bonds are effectively lending money to the issuing company in exchange for periodic interest payments and the return of the principal amount at maturity.

Types of Corporate Bonds in India

  1. Public Issue Corporate Bonds: These bonds are offered to the public through a formal issuance process and are typically listed on stock exchanges for trading.
  2. Private Placement Corporate Bonds: Issued directly to a select group of investors without a public offering, often involving institutional investors.
  3. Outstanding Corporate Bonds: These refer to bonds that have been issued and are currently held by investors, with details available through regulatory bodies.

Benefits of Investing in Corporate Bonds

  • Fixed Income: Corporate bonds provide regular interest payments, offering a predictable income stream.
  • Capital Preservation: Bonds are generally considered safer than equities, with a promise of principal repayment at maturity.
  • Diversification: Including corporate bonds in an investment portfolio can help diversify risk.
  • Tax Benefits: Certain bonds may offer tax advantages under Indian tax laws.

Risks Associated with Corporate Bonds

  • Credit Risk: The possibility that the issuing company may default on interest or principal payments.
  • Interest Rate Risk: Changes in interest rates can affect the market value of existing bonds.
  • Liquidity Risk: Some corporate bonds may not be easily tradable in the secondary market.

How to Invest in Corporate Bonds

Investors can purchase corporate bonds through:

  • Primary Market: During the initial issuance phase via investment banks or brokers.
  • Secondary Market: Through stock exchanges like NSE or BSE, using a demat and trading account.
  • Bond Central Portal: A centralized database launched by SEBI for accessing information on corporate bonds.

Regulatory Framework

The Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) regulates the corporate bond market in India. SEBI's guidelines ensure transparency, investor protection, and efficient functioning of the bond market.

Recent Developments

SEBI launched 'Bond Central', a centralized database portal for corporate bonds, aiming to enhance transparency and accessibility for investors.

Tip: Before investing in corporate bonds, always check the credit rating of the issuing company to assess the risk level associated with the investment.

Real Estate Investment in India

What Is Real Estate Investment in India?

Real estate investment in India involves purchasing property—residential, commercial, or land—with the expectation of earning returns through rental income, capital appreciation, or both. The sector is a significant contributor to India's GDP and employment, driven by rapid urbanization, infrastructural development, and a growing middle class.

Types of Real Estate Investment

  1. Physical Property Investment: Direct purchase of residential or commercial properties for rental income or capital appreciation.
  2. Real Estate Investment Trusts (REITs): Investment in publicly traded trusts that own and manage income-generating real estate assets. REITs offer liquidity and diversification with lower capital requirements compared to direct property investment.
  3. Real Estate Mutual Funds: Funds that pool money from investors to invest in a diversified portfolio of real estate-related assets, including REITs and property stocks.
  4. Real Estate Crowdfunding: A method where multiple investors pool funds to invest in specific real estate projects, allowing for fractional ownership.

Benefits of Real Estate Investment

  • Capital Appreciation: Properties in prime locations tend to increase in value over time, providing substantial returns on investment.
  • Rental Income: Regular income through leasing properties to tenants, offering a steady cash flow.
  • Portfolio Diversification: Real estate investments can reduce overall portfolio risk by providing exposure to a different asset class.
  • Tax Benefits: Deductions on home loan interest and principal repayment under sections 24(b) and 80C of the Income Tax Act.

Risks Associated with Real Estate Investment

  • Market Volatility: Property values can fluctuate based on economic conditions, interest rates, and demand-supply dynamics.
  • Liquidity Risk: Selling property can be time-consuming and may not yield immediate returns.
  • Regulatory Risks: Changes in government policies, zoning laws, or taxation can impact property values and rental yields.
  • Maintenance Costs: Ongoing expenses for property upkeep, repairs, and management can affect profitability.

How to Invest in Real Estate in India

  • Direct Purchase: Buy property through real estate developers or resale markets. Ensure due diligence on legal titles, approvals, and builder credibility.
  • REITs: Invest in listed REITs through stock exchanges. Platforms like ICICI Direct facilitate REIT investments in India.
  • Real Estate Mutual Funds: Invest via asset management companies offering funds focused on real estate sectors.
  • Crowdfunding Platforms: Engage with platforms that offer fractional investment opportunities in specific real estate projects.

Recent Trends in Indian Real Estate

  • Institutional Investment Growth: In 2024, institutional investments in India's real estate sector reached a record USD 8.9 billion, marking a 51% increase from the previous year.
  • Urbanization and Housing Demand: By 2036, 39% of India's population is projected to live in urban areas, creating an additional housing demand of 64 million units.
  • Luxury Segment Growth: There is a growing demand for luxury properties, leading to an increase in home prices and rental costs, particularly in major cities.

Note: Real estate investment requires thorough research and due diligence. Always consult with legal and financial experts before making significant property investments.

Gold

Gold has always held a significant place in Indian culture and traditions for centuries. But today, gold has also emerged as a popular investment avenue in India. The country has a long-standing affinity for this precious metal and it plays a crucial role in the country's economic landscape.

While gold has always been an investment choice for the elderly, the new age methods to invest in gold also made it a popular investment choice among millennials. Let's explore the reasons why considering gold as an investment in India is a prudent choice, the different ways to invest in gold and tax implications on buying gold.

Why You Should Invest in Gold?

If you are searching for reasons to invest in this precious metal, then here are a few of them:

1) Historical Stability

Gold has always proven to be a reliable store of value over the years. Gold's value has withstood economic downturns while also being a hedge against inflation. This makes it a safe haven during times of crisis.

2) Diversification

Gold as an investment serves as a diversification tool for investors. By including gold in your portfolio, you can mitigate risks like extreme market volatility. Gold prices are not expected to be much volatile and therefore, are ideally suited for risk-averse investors.

3) Hedging Against Inflation

Gold has historically acted as a hedge against inflation. When inflation rises, the value of paper currency tends to decline but gold often retains its value. Further, it has been successful in delivering returns higher than the inflation rate thus making it a suitable hedge.

4) Cultural Significance

In India, gold is not considered just an investment but a symbol of wealth and prosperity. Gold jewellery is an integral part of Indian weddings and festivals. Further, the festival season in India witnesses a significant surge in gold demand, especially during Diwali.

5) Global Acceptance

Gold is a universally accepted form of asset. You can easily convert gold into cash anywhere in the world. This provides much liquidity and global mobility to investors.

How to Invest in Gold?

Gone are the days when you needed to visit your jeweller to invest in gold. With the advent of technology, multiple gold investment options have emerged. Here are the different ways on how to invest in gold in India today:

1) Physical Gold

Physical gold investment is basically holding gold in tangible form. You can buy gold jewellery and ornaments that will also be helpful on occasions and festivals. Alternatively, you can invest in gold coins and bars if you are buying with the sole purpose of investment. One of the major drawbacks of physical gold investment is that it proves to be costlier due to making, designing and labour charges. Also, there is a risk of theft due to which you might end up paying bank locker charges as well.

2) Digital Gold

There are investment platforms that allow digital gold investment. When you invest your money in digital gold, the investment company buys an equivalent amount of gold on your behalf and stores it in secure vaults. The gold is insured by the insurance companies thereby saving you all the hassle of going to your jeweller or from the risk of theft and other charges.

3) Gold Funds

Also known as gold mutual funds, it's a gold investment plan that pool money from various investors and invest in various forms of gold. The fund tracks the prices of gold and offers the same gold investment returns.

4) Gold ETFs (Exchange-Traded Funds)

It's akin to stock market investment but for gold. Gold ETFs trade on stock exchanges that provide you an opportunity to buy and sell gold, just like stocks.

Tax Benefits on Gold Investments

Another reason as to why invest in gold is taxes. The government also promotes gold as an investment and thus, offers multiple tax benefits on gold investments. These include:

  • Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG) Tax Exemption: If you hold gold for more than 3 years, gains from selling your gold investments are exempt from LTCG tax up to Rs. 1 lakh. Further, capital gains on sovereign gold bonds and gold monetization schemes are exempted.
  • Indexation Benefit: You can benefit from indexation to adjust the purchase price for inflation. This directly reduces your capital gains tax.
  • 80C Deduction: Investing in Sovereign Gold Bonds provides you a deduction of up to Rs. 1.50 lakhs for each financial year.
  • Exempt Interest: Interest on the gold monetization scheme is exempted from tax.

Gold Savings Plans

Regular gold savings plans allow customers to build gold holdings by making periodic purchases, for example on a weekly or monthly basis. Providers of gold savings plans safely store customers' gold on their behalf and these plans are typically targeted at mass market consumers because both minimum investments and regular savings amounts are low.

Customers pursue different objectives with gold savings plans. Some customers use them to increase their gold holdings in small, regular increments. As such, they focus on the financial benefits of gold as an asset class, using it as a foundational asset, a savings tool or a means of diversification and risk mitigation. The cost average effect of small, regular investments provides an additional benefit, since the investment is accumulated incrementally over time, the investor is less exposed to short-term price fluctuations.

Systematic Investment Plan (SIP)

What is a Systematic Investment Plan (SIP)?

A Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) is a disciplined and convenient way to invest in mutual funds. It allows investors to contribute a fixed amount at regular intervals—monthly, quarterly, etc.—into a selected mutual fund scheme. SIPs are ideal for individuals aiming to build long-term wealth while avoiding the need to time the market.

Benefits of SIP

  1. Disciplined Investment

    SIPs cultivate consistent investing habits by automating regular contributions. This eliminates emotional decision-making and the temptation to time the market.

  2. Rupee Cost Averaging

    Investing at regular intervals, regardless of market conditions, helps average out the cost of mutual fund units. This reduces the impact of market volatility over time.

    Example:

    • Month 1: NAV = ₹50 → Units bought = 100 (₹5,000 / ₹50)
    • Month 2: NAV = ₹40 → Units bought = 125
    • Month 3: NAV = ₹45 → Units bought = 111.11
    • Total Investment = ₹15,000
    • Total Units Accumulated = 336.11
    • Average Cost per Unit = ₹44.64

    This illustrates how rupee cost averaging can lead to more units purchased at a lower average cost.

  3. Power of Compounding

    SIPs harness the power of compounding, where returns are reinvested to generate further earnings. Starting early and staying invested multiplies wealth over time.

    Example (at 12% annual returns):

    • After 10 years: ₹6 lakhs invested → ₹22.6 lakhs
    • After 20 years: ₹12 lakhs invested → ₹50 lakhs
    • After 30 years: ₹18 lakhs invested → ₹1.76 crores
  4. Affordability

    SIPs are accessible to all, with some schemes starting at just ₹500 per month, making it suitable for investors across income levels.

  5. Convenience and Flexibility

    Setting up a SIP is simple. You can choose the amount, frequency, and duration. Automated bank deductions make the process seamless.

How SIP Works

  1. Choose a Mutual Fund Scheme

    Pick a fund aligned with your financial goals and risk profile.

  2. Decide the SIP Amount

    Determine a comfortable amount to invest regularly.

  3. Select Investment Frequency

    Monthly, quarterly, or any interval of your choice.

  4. Automate Bank Deductions

    Your SIP amount is auto-debited from your bank account and invested at the prevailing NAV.

  5. Units Allocation

    The money gets converted into mutual fund units and added to your investment portfolio.

  6. Track Performance

    Monitor and revise your SIP as per your goals and market performance.

Types of SIP

  • Fixed SIP

    Invests a fixed amount at regular intervals—best for those with a steady income.

  • Flexible SIP

    Allows modification of investment amounts depending on your financial situation.

  • Top-Up SIP

    Periodically increase the investment amount, keeping pace with income growth or inflation.

  • Perpetual SIP

    Has no end date, letting you continue investments until you choose to stop—ideal for long-term planning.

  • Trigger SIP

    Initiates or modifies investments based on predefined market conditions. Suitable for seasoned investors.

Is SIP Safe?

Yes, SIPs are a safe and structured way to invest in mutual funds. They help avoid market timing by investing regularly across market cycles. This strategy, called rupee cost averaging, ensures you do not pay a high lump sum price during market peaks.

Are SIP Returns Taxable?

Yes, but it depends on the type of fund and holding period.

  • Equity Funds:
    • Redeemed after 1 year: No tax (LTCG up to ₹1 lakh is tax-free)
    • Redeemed within 1 year: 15% tax (Short-Term Capital Gains)
  • Debt Funds:
    • Redeemed within 3 years: Taxed as per your income slab
    • Redeemed after 3 years: 20% tax with indexation benefits

Note: Each SIP installment is considered a separate investment for tax purposes.

Example:

If you start a ₹1,000 SIP in January 2018 and continue till December 2018, each installment has its own 12-month clock. To avoid taxes, the January 2018 SIP must be redeemed after January 2019, and so on.

Can a SIP Be Stopped?

Yes. SIPs can be stopped at any time—unlike FDs or RDs. You may either:

  • Redeem your units
  • Let the invested amount continue to grow in the fund

Can SIP Help Save Tax?

Yes, if invested in ELSS (Equity Linked Saving Scheme) mutual funds. Under Section 80C, you can claim tax deductions up to ₹1.5 lakhs per financial year.

Example 1:

SIP of ₹12,500/month from April 2018 to March 2019 = ₹1.5 lakh invested
→ Full tax benefit for FY 2018–19

Example 2:

SIP of ₹12,500/month starting from May 2018 to April 2019 = ₹1,37,500 invested in FY 2018–19
→ Tax deduction only on ₹1,37,500

Fixed Deposit

Fixed deposits are the most popular investment instruments in India. For years, investing has been synonymous with FDs since this product is considered safe, provides guaranteed returns over a period of time, and is also flexible in its duration.

  • No matter how interest rates move or economy performs, you will get the returns fixed at the time of investing.
  • You earn interest on the interest amount too. This helps you accumulate a big amount with ease.

What is a Fixed Deposit?

A fixed deposit or an FD is an investment instrument that banks and non-banking financial companies (NBFC) offer their customers. Through an FD, people invest a certain sum of money for a fixed period at a predetermined rate of interest in an FD. The rate of interest varies from one financial institution to another, although it is usually higher than the interest offered on savings accounts.

Fixed deposits are available for different periods, ranging from very short-term tenures of 7-14 days to long tenures of 10 years. A fixed deposit is sometimes known as a term deposit.

How Does a Fixed Deposit Work?

You may think of a fixed deposit as lending money to a bank or an NBFC. When you invest in an FD, the financial institution guarantees to return the invested sum at the end of the tenure, known as the maturity period, and pays you interest for it. The bank may use this money to lend to other borrowers and charges them an interest for the same. A portion of this interest is passed on to you.

The interest offered depends on the tenure or maturity period of the FD. A 7-day fixed deposit will carry a lower annual interest rate compared to a one-year FD. This is to compensate for the time-risk of money. Simply put, a rupee today is more valuable than the same rupee a year from now. This is because inflation pushes up prices over time. A rupee will buy you more goods today than it will a year from now. An investor needs to be compensated for this.

You can choose to reinvest the interest or receive an interest amount periodically in your bank account.

  1. Cumulative FDs pay you the interest and the principal at maturity. The interest is reinvested every year. This means that you will not be eligible to receive regular interest payouts, instead of receiving a lump sum at the end of the FD tenure. The cumulative FD option may be suitable for you if you do not need a regular stream of income. Under this option, you will also benefit from the power of compounding, as the following year's interest will be calculated on the principal plus interest of the previous year.
  2. Non-cumulative FDs will pay you interest at fixed intervals. You could choose to receive interest payments monthly, quarterly, half-yearly, or annually, depending upon your needs. This will give you a regular stream of income. However, the downside of non-cumulative FDs is that you will lose out on earning interest on interest.

Benefits of FDs

Fixed deposits have many benefits such as:

  • Assured Return: Unlike market-linked securities that may result in losses due to market volatility, fixed deposits provide an assured rate of return on investments. Your capital remains safe in FDs, and returns are higher than savings accounts.
  • Compounding: With FD investments, you can earn interest on interest, thereby enjoying higher returns and faster multiplication of money.
  • Low Minimum Investment: If you want to inculcate an investment habit but do not have a large sum to do so, FDs are a good option because investment amounts can start as low as Rs. 500.
  • Liquidity: Premature withdrawal of FDs is permitted, although you will lose some interest in the missed duration. However, this gives you the benefit of liquidity since you can liquidate the FD in times of emergencies.
  • Easy Process: FDs are the easiest instruments to invest in, both offline and online, through net banking or mobile banking.
  • Higher Rates for Senior Citizens: Senior citizens can earn more from their life's savings and move one step closer to no-compromise retired life.

How Is Interest on FDs Calculated?

Calculating the interest earned on fixed deposits is a function of the amount invested, the rate of interest being offered, and the duration of the investment. The basic formula to calculate interest on your FD is:

Interest on FD = Amount Invested x Interest Rate x (Duration/ 12 months)

However, depending upon whether you choose a cumulative or non-cumulative FD, the interest amount will vary. For instance, if you choose to invest ₹25,000 for 3 years at an interest rate of 7.1% per annum, a cumulative FD would have a maturity value of ₹30,712.

Who Should Invest in an FD?

When you invest in a fixed deposit, the duration of investment or tenure and interest amount is predetermined. The institution also assures you to return your money at maturity. That makes FDs a relatively safe investment avenue. Fixed deposits are a good investment option for:

  • Conservative Investors: People with a low-risk appetite who want to invest but still cannot stomach high risk. FDs provide a higher return than money kept in the savings account.
  • Investor with Short-Term Goals: Fixed deposits (FDs) are ideal for investors with short-term goals because they offer guaranteed returns and a fixed interest rate, ensuring stability and predictability. Unlike market-linked investments, FDs have almost no risk, making them a safe choice for achieving near-term financial objectives.
  • Balancing the risk in an overall portfolio: Even for those with a medium-to-high risk appetite, investing a portion of your funds in fixed deposits balances out the risk from market-linked instruments like equity or mutual funds.
  • Retired Individuals: Retired individuals who want to ensure the stability of investment and earn guaranteed returns can also invest in FDs.

PPF (Public Provident Fund)

What is PPF?

The Public Provident Fund (PPF) is a long term, government backed savings scheme designed to offer secure and stable returns. It encourages individuals to invest periodically with the primary goal of preserving capital while earning compounded interest.

Upon opening a PPF account, deposits can be made monthly (or in lump sum) and the invested amount accrues interest, compounded annually. The scheme has a fixed tenure and strict rules governing withdrawals and loans.

Key Features

Feature Details
Interest Rate 7.1% per annum (subject to quarterly revision)
Tenure 15 years (extendable in 5 year blocks)
Minimum Annual Deposit ₹ 500
Maximum Annual Deposit ₹ 1.5 lakh (per financial year)
Risk Profile Capital guaranteed; not market linked
Tax Benefits Exempt under Sections 80C and EEE

Tip: Investing each year keeps the account active and eligible for interest; missed years earn no interest.

Principal Amount & Deposits

A minimum of Rs. 500 and a maximum of Rs. 1.5 Lakh can be invested in a provident fund scheme annually. This investment can be undertaken on a lump sum or installment basis. However, an individual is eligible for only 12 yearly instalment payments into a PPF account. Investment in a PPF account has to be made every year to ensure that the account remains active.

Loans Against PPF

Public provident funds provide the benefit of availing loans against the investment amount. However, the loan will only be granted if it is taken at any time from the beginning of 3rd year till the end of the 6th year from the date of activation of the account. The maximum tenure of such loans against PPF is 36 months. Only 25% or less of the total amount available in the account can be claimed for this purpose.

Eligibility Criteria

  • Residents: Indian citizens (including minors, with parental operation).
  • Non Resident Indians: Cannot open new accounts; existing accounts remain active until maturity but cannot be extended.

Interest Calculation

The interest payable on public provident fund schemes is determined by the Central Government of India. It aims to provide higher interest than regular accounts maintained by various commercial banks in the country. Interest rates currently payable on such accounts stand at 7.1%, and are subject to quarterly updates at the discretion of the government.

How to Open a PPF Account

  1. Choose a Bank or Post Office: Visit their branch or online portal.
  2. Submit KYC Documents:
    • Proof of Identity (Aadhaar, Voter ID, Driving Licence)
    • PAN Card
    • Proof of Address
    • Passport size Photograph
    • Nominee Declaration Form
  3. Initial Deposit: Minimum ₹ 500.

Tax Benefits

Income tax exemptions are applicable on the principal amount invested in a PPF as an account. The entire value of investment can be claimed for tax waiver under section 80C of the Income Tax Act of 1961. However, it should be kept in mind that the total principal that can be invested in one financial year cannot exceed Rs. 1.5 Lakh. The total interest accrued on PPF investment is also exempt from any tax calculations.

Therefore, the entire amount redeemed from a PPF account upon completion of maturity is not subject to taxation. This policy makes the public provident fund scheme attractive to many investors in India.

Withdrawal Rules

  • Before 5 Years: No withdrawals except in specified hardship cases.
  • After 5 Years:
    • One partial withdrawal per year, up to 50% of balance at end of Year 4.
  • At Maturity (15 Years): Full withdrawal of principal and interest.

Loan vs. Withdrawal Comparison

Aspect Loan (Years 3–6) Partial Withdrawal (Year 6+)
Max Amount 25% of 2 year old balance 50% of Year 4 balance
Repayment Within 36 months N/A (no repayment required)
Impact on Interest Interest continues on full balance Withdrawn amount stops earning interest

Note: PPF is best suited for risk averse savers seeking tax efficient, long term growth. Always verify the latest interest rates and rules before investing.

National Pension Scheme (NPS)

What is the National Pension Scheme?

The National Pension Scheme (NPS) is a government-backed social security initiative designed to provide retirement income to all Indian citizens, including employees in the public, private and unorganised sectors, except members of the armed forces. Subscribers invest regularly in a pension account during their working life and receive a lump-sum withdrawal and a monthly pension upon retirement.

Key Features & Particulars

Particular Details
Scheme Name NPS Vatsalya Pension Scheme
Minimum Entry Age 18 years
Maximum Entry Age 65 years
Contribution Flexibility Any time during financial year; unlimited
Equity Exposure Cap 50%–75% (50% for government employees)
Annuity Purchase 40% of corpus
Lump sum Withdrawal at Retirement Up to 60% of accumulated corpus
Pension Start Age From 60 years
Tax Benefits Under Sections 80C, 80CCD and related clauses

Who Should Invest in NPS?

  • Individuals seeking a disciplined, low risk retirement savings plan.
  • Salaried employees (especially private sector) aiming for a regular post retirement income.
  • Self-employed professionals looking to maximise tax deductions under Section 80C.
  • Anyone who wants to diversify beyond traditional fixed income tax saving instruments.

Benefits of NPS

Returns & Interest

  • Historically delivered 9%–12% annualised returns by allocating a portion to equity and debt.
  • Equity exposure can be rebalanced automatically (reducing by 2.5% annually after age 50) to manage risk.
  • Option to switch fund managers if performance is unsatisfactory.

Risk Management

  • Equity cap (50%–75%) ensures exposure is controlled; reduces volatility.
  • Government employee equity cap fixed at 50% beyond age 60.
  • Debt allocation stabilises the portfolio and safeguards the corpus.

Regulation & Transparency

  • Overseen by PFRDA with clear investment norms and regular performance audits.
  • Monitoring of fund managers by NPS Trust.

Flexibility & Portability

  • Unlimited contributions at any time during the year.
  • Choice of investment options (auto choice or active choice).
  • Continue the same NPS account across employers and locations.

Tax Benefits

Employee Contributions (Self)

  • Deduction up to 10% of salary (Basic + DA) under Section 80CCD(1), within the overall ₹1.5 lakh limit of Section 80CCE.
  • Additional deduction up to ₹50,000 under Section 80CCD(1B) (over and above the ₹1.5 lakh limit).

Employer Contributions

  • Deduction up to 14% of salary (Basic + DA) under Section 80CCD(2) for Central Government employees (increased from 10% effective 1 April 2025).
  • Employer contribution not counted within the ₹1.5 lakh Section 80C/80CCE ceiling.

Self Employed Contributions

  • Deduction up to 20% of gross income under Section 80CCD(1), subject to the ₹1.5 lakh cap of Section 80CCE.
  • Additional ₹50,000 deduction under Section 80CCD(1B).

Partial Withdrawals

  • Tax free up to 25% of own contributions under Section 10(12B), subject to PFRDA rules.

Annuity Purchase & Lump sum

  • Amount used for annuity (≥ 40% of corpus) exempt under Section 80CCD(5).
  • Subsequent pension income taxed as per individual’s slab under Section 80CCD(3).
  • Lump sum withdrawal of 60% of corpus at retirement exempt under Section 10.

Corporate/Employer Tax Breaks

  • Employer contributions (up to 10% of salary for non-Government employers) deductible as business expense under Section 36(1)(iv)(a).

Withdrawal Rules at Retirement (Age 60+)

  • Lump Sum: Up to 60% of corpus can be withdrawn tax free.
  • Annuity: Remaining 40% must be used to purchase an annuity; pension proceeds taxable as per slab.
  • Low Corpus Exit: If total corpus ≤ ₹5 lakh, 100% lump sum withdrawal allowed (tax free).

Example: With a ₹4.5 lakh corpus, you may fully withdraw the lump sum. If corpus exceeds ₹10 lakh, only ₹6 lakh is tax free; the rest requires annuity purchase.

Early Exit & Superannuation Rules

  • Superannuation (At 60): Minimum 40% annuitisation; balance withdrawn.
  • Premature Exit (Before 60):
    • ≥ 80% annuitisation of corpus required.
    • If corpus ≤ ₹2.5 lakh, 100% lump sum withdrawal permitted.
  • On Subscriber’s Death: 100% of accrued corpus paid to nominee/legal heir.

Note: Scheme provisions are subject to PFRDA guidelines and may change. Always verify current rules before investing.

Emergency Fund

What Is an Emergency Fund?

An emergency fund is a dedicated savings reserve set aside to cover unexpected expenses, such as medical bills, car repairs, or job loss. It's a financial safety net that helps you manage unforeseen financial challenges without resorting to high-interest debt or disrupting long-term savings plans.

Why Is an Emergency Fund Important?

Having an emergency fund provides financial stability and peace of mind. It ensures that you can handle life's unexpected events without derailing your financial goals or accumulating debt. It's especially crucial in times of economic uncertainty or personal emergencies.

How Much Should You Save in an Emergency Fund?

Financial experts typically recommend saving three to six months' worth of essential living expenses. This amount can vary based on individual circumstances, such as job stability, health considerations, and personal financial obligations.

Where Should You Keep Your Emergency Fund?

It's advisable to store your emergency fund in a liquid and low-risk account. Options include:

  • High-yield savings accounts
  • Money market accounts
  • Liquid mutual funds

These options offer easy access to funds while earning some interest, ensuring your money is both safe and accessible when needed.

How to Build an Emergency Fund?

Building an emergency fund involves:

  1. Setting a Goal: Determine the amount you aim to save based on your monthly expenses.
  2. Creating a Budget: Identify areas where you can cut back to allocate more towards your emergency fund.
  3. Automating Savings: Set up automatic transfers to your emergency fund to ensure consistent saving.
  4. Starting Small: Begin with manageable amounts and gradually increase your savings as your financial situation improves.

When Should You Use Your Emergency Fund?

Your emergency fund should be reserved for genuine emergencies, such as:

  • Unplanned medical expenses
  • Urgent home or car repairs
  • Temporary loss of income

It's important to distinguish between true emergencies and non-essential expenses to maintain the integrity of your emergency fund.

How Can You Replenish Your Emergency Fund?

If you need to use your emergency fund, focus on replenishing it as soon as possible. Strategies include:

  • Reducing discretionary spending
  • Increasing income through side jobs or freelance work
  • Allocating any windfalls, such as tax refunds or bonuses, towards your emergency fund

What Are the Benefits of Having an Emergency Fund?

  • Financial Security: Provides a cushion against unexpected expenses.
  • Debt Prevention: Reduces the need to rely on credit cards or loans during emergencies.
  • Peace of Mind: Knowing you have funds available can reduce stress during challenging times.

Tip: Start with a smaller goal of saving ₹10,000 or one month's expenses before working toward the full 3-6 month recommendation. Even a small emergency fund provides significant financial protection.
